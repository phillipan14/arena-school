The setup

The second cohort, at the International School of Beijing.

Arena's second cohort ran in the summer of 2026, at the International School of Beijing, for students in grades 7 through 12. The same premise as Hong Kong the year before: a real problem, a mentor, five days, no patch on the old architecture.

What students built

Every team shipped a live product. With real users.

Not a slide deck. Not a prototype that only works in the demo. Every team in the cohort shipped something live, with real users on the other end. Somewhere in that week, before the pitch and before the prize, the first real revenue came in.

The demo day

Judged by founders and operators from Harvard, Y Combinator, and leading AI companies.

The week closed with a final pitch for a ¥8,888 prize, judged by founders and operators from Harvard, Y Combinator, and leading AI companies. Not teachers grading a rubric. People who fund and build companies for a living, deciding which product was actually worth backing.

What happened after

Six of six asked to continue.

Six students answered the post-camp survey. All six asked to continue. The School-Year Mentorship Program is now running for alumni: the companies that started as a five-day sprint keep going, with mentorship instead of a countdown clock.
