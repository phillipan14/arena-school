How it runs

Taken apart on day one. Made their own on day two.

  1. Day 1 · morning
    Take it apart Agency

    Students pick an app and map how it works: the screens, the buttons, and what each tap does.

  2. Day 1 · afternoon
    Build the core Taste

    They describe each screen to AI tools, test what comes back, and get the main feature working.

  3. Day 2
    Add a twist, launch Craft

    They add what the original is missing, test with classmates, and publish at a link anyone can open.

Fair questions

What students, parents, and schools ask.

Do students need to know how to code?+

No. Students describe what they want in plain language, test what the AI tools produce, and decide what stays.

Is it just a copy of the real app?+

No. Students rebuild the core idea to learn how it works, then make it their own with a new name, their own design, and a feature the original doesn’t have.

Won’t this mean more screen time?+

It’s a different kind of screen time. Students plan on paper, test with classmates, and spend the day making something instead of scrolling.

Which AI tools do students use?+

Whatever your school approves, including tools that work in China. The skill is describing what they want and judging the result, so it isn’t tied to one product.

How much does it cost?+

It depends on group size, length, and format, which we shape with each school. Tell us about yours and we’ll share options.

What does the school provide?+

A room, a laptop for each student, and a teacher who co-runs the days with us. We bring the plan and the materials.
