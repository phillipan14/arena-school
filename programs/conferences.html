What organizers need

Four needs, four answers.

What conference organizers and educator networks ask for, and how each session is shaped.

  1. The need

    Something usable the next school day

    Attendees leave many sessions with ideas but nothing they can use.

    How we answer it

    Hands-on, on their own material

    Attendees use AI on their own lessons and materials, with help in the room, and keep the prompts and templates.

  2. The need

    Many subjects and roles in one room

    A session has to work for a science teacher and a head of school alike.

    How we answer it

    Three tracks to start from

    Classroom practice, teaching and assessment, and leadership and parents, shaped around attendees’ subjects.

  3. The need

    Balance, not hype

    Educators hear both alarm and promises about AI.

    How we answer it

    Where it helps and where it falls short

    Balanced guidance, including how teachers keep decisions about teaching and assessment.

  4. The need

    A format that fits the program

    Keynote slots, breakouts, and panels all need different things.

    How we answer it

    Four formats

    A keynote or talk, a breakout workshop, a panel, or a pre-conference workshop, each shaped with the organizer.

What the research says

Teachers are already using AI. The rules have not caught up.

Four findings from recent studies. Each links to its source.

  • 5.9hours

    a week saved, by their own estimate, among teachers who use AI at least weekly: about six weeks over a school year.

    Source: Gallup and Walton Family Foundation, survey of 2,232 U.S. public K-12 teachers, spring 2025

  • 31%

    less lesson-planning time for teachers in an English trial who used ChatGPT with a guide. An expert panel found no difference in quality.

    Source: Education Endowment Foundation trial, evaluated by NFER, reported March 2025

  • 53%

    of English, math, and science teachers said they used AI for school in 2025, up more than 15 points in one to two years.

    Source: RAND, AI Use in Schools Is Quickly Increasing but Guidance Lags Behind, September 2025

  • 34%

    of teachers reported school or district policies on AI use related to academic integrity.

    Source: RAND, AI Use in Schools Is Quickly Increasing but Guidance Lags Behind, September 2025

Sample workshops

Ideas to start from, in three tracks.

Each is a starting point. We shape every session around your attendees’ subjects and questions.

Classroom practice

Hands-on, for any subject.

  • Report comments in each teacher’s voice
    Drafts based on their past comments, which the teacher edits and approves.
  • One text, several reading levels
    The same text for English learners, learning support, on-level, and advanced readers.
  • From exit tickets to reteach groups
    Sorting common misconceptions and planning a short reteach.

Teaching and assessment

For teachers deciding how AI fits their assessment.

  • Redesigning an assignment
    Making AI use part of the learning, with reasoning assessed.
  • Deciding what to assess
    A framework applied to a real unit.
  • Assessing the process
    Oral defenses, visible drafts, and AI transcripts in the submission.

Leadership and parents

A talk and open conversation.

  • Working Beside AI: What It Means for Our Kids’ Futures
    How work is changing and what students can already make, followed by questions.

Questions

Common questions

How is a session planned?+

We agree the topic, audience, and length with you, then build the examples around your attendees’ subjects, so each educator works on material they already teach.

Which AI tools do attendees use?+

Tools that suit your audience, including ones that work in China. We teach a method rather than one product, so it carries over to whatever tools attendees use next.

How much does it cost?+

It depends on the format and length. Tell us about your event, audience, and dates, and we will reply within three working days with options.

Can sessions run in Chinese?+

Yes. Sessions run in English or Chinese.
