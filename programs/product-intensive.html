5-Day Product Intensive

A five-day program at your school. Student teams choose a problem, build a product with AI tools, publish it at a public link, and present it to a panel.

  • Grades7 to 12
  • Group15 to 25 students
  • LengthFive days
  • CodingNone needed
  • LanguageEnglish or Chinese
Contact us →Get the one-pager
What students do

One product per team, from problem to presentation.

Students work in small teams with mentors in the room. Each day ends with a short demo.

01

Choose a problem

Students pitch problems for three minutes each, with no slides. Teams form around the ones they care about most.

02

Build and test

Short lessons just before they are needed, then long build blocks and feedback from users.

03

Launch and present

Products are live at a public link by day three. On day five, each team presents to a panel.

How it runs

The week, day by day.

  1. 01 · Day 1

    Choose a problem

    Three-minute pitches, then teams form.

  2. 02 · Day 2

    Talk to users

    Interviews first, then the first build.

  3. 03 · Day 3

    Live at a link

    A working version anyone can open.

  4. 04 · Day 4

    Brand, price, freeze

    The build stops at a fixed hour.

  5. 05 · Day 5

    Demo day

    Each team presents to a panel. Families are invited.

Your teachers co-run every day with us and keep the format to run again.

Questions

Common questions

Do students need to know how to code?+

No. Students describe what they want in plain language, test what the AI tools produce, and decide what stays.

How are teams formed?+

Around the problems students pitch on day one. Each student chooses the team they join.

Who judges demo day?+

A panel of founders and operators. In Beijing, judges came from Harvard, Y Combinator, and AI labs.

What do our teachers do?+

They co-run every day with us and sit with teams while they build.

Which AI tools do students use?+

Whatever your school approves, including tools that work in China. The skill is describing what they want and judging the result.

Is there a shorter version?+

Yes. A two-to-three-day starter covers choosing a problem, building, and a short demo.
