What program operators weigh

Four concerns, one track.

What camp operators, bootcamps, and enrichment brands ask before adding a track, and how it is set up.

  1. The concern

    Families want AI, experts are hard to staff

    Building a credible AI program means finding people who have built real products.

    How the track handles it

    Led by a co-founder

    An Arena co-founder leads the track, with guest speakers and judges from leading universities and companies.

  2. The concern

    Your brand is on the line

    Families trust your name, and an outside partner should not get in the way.

    How the track handles it

    Your brand stays in front

    Families see your brand throughout, and your team keeps full control of recruiting, venue, and operations.

  3. The concern

    Families want to see results

    A week of activities is hard to show at the end.

    How the track handles it

    A product they can show

    Students choose a real problem and finish a product; products stay live after the program.

  4. The concern

    Terms need to be clear

    A new track has to fit your budget and your enrollment.

    How the track handles it

    Agreed up front

    Fixed fee, revenue share, or a hybrid, agreed before the program, with enrollment reviewed together.

The track

What your students walk away with.

For camp operators, bootcamps, and enrichment brands: students choose a real problem, build with AI tools, and finish a product they can show.

  1. The core module

    The product track

    Problem selection, user research, building with AI coding tools, brand, pricing, and a final pitch to judges.

  2. The teaching team

    Experienced mentors

    Led by an Arena co-founder, with guest speakers and judges from leading universities and companies.

  3. The follow-through

    After the program

    Products stay live after the program, and optional mentorship keeps students working on them.
