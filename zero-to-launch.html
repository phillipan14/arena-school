Getting a job just got harder

For the year when your resume has to start proving things.

For college and masters students applying to internships and first jobs. For students with an offer who want to arrive dangerous on day one. For anyone whose resume says "proficient in AI" with nothing to show for it.

12M+

record college graduates entering the job market in China alone this year

1 in 2

occupations already use AI for a quarter or more of their tasks

First

entry-level work is what automates first: the jobs new graduates apply to

The interview problem, stated plainly: every candidate now claims AI skills. Interviewers have stopped believing it. The candidate who opens a laptop and demos something real is playing a different game.

A true story we teach from: a candidate for an international growth role automated the job's social workflows before the interview, then showed the interviewer the script running live. She got the job. You will build your own version.

Where this takes you

Pick the career. Build the project that gets you hired into it.

The same seven days build the same skills for everyone: research, building with AI, design, storytelling, shipping. What changes is the project you point them at. The target-job build is the artifact made for your exact role.

Consulting

Skills: structured problem solving, research synthesis, deck craft with AI

Sample build: an AI research agent that turns a messy industry question into a structured briefing deck.

Finance and banking

Skills: data analysis with AI, modeling workflows, precision under deadline

Sample build: an automation that pulls company filings and drafts a first-pass comparables analysis.

Product management

Skills: user research, PRDs, prioritization, shipping with engineers (human or AI)

Sample build: the whole bootcamp is this job: a live product from interviews to launch, with the PRD to show for it.

Marketing and growth

Skills: brand, content generation, ad creative, channel experiments

Sample build: an automation for a real growth workflow: social content, outreach, or ad variants. This build got one candidate her offer at a leading AI company.

Data and research

Skills: analysis pipelines, qualitative clustering, honest interpretation

Sample build: a pipeline that ingests survey or usage data and outputs charts plus a written readout for your manager.

Founder

Skills: all of the above, plus pricing, first revenue, and the courage to charge

Sample build: keep your bootcamp product alive after the week ends. Some students have; one team registered a company.

Day by day

A representative schedule, refined with the cohort.

Accepted students receive a pre-work packet before the cohort starts, so the thinking begins before day one. Company visits and guest firesides slot in across the build days; exact days flex with who's available that week.

  1. Day 01
    Arrival, problem selection, theme review

    Cohort arrives. You get feedback on your pre-work, then pick your theme or problem.

  2. Day 02
    User research, interviews, PRD

    Structured user research, real interviews with real people, a mini-PRD with the features prioritized.

  3. Day 03
    Build, with coding agents

    AI coding agents build the product while you learn to direct them. Visit / fireside slot

  4. Day 04
    Brand, landing page, advertisement

    The product gets a name, a design system, a landing page, and an advertisement. Visit / fireside slot

  5. Day 05
    Launch, pricing, real users, build freeze

    The product goes live, gets priced, and finds its first real users. The build freezes at a fixed hour: what's live is what demos. Visit / fireside slot

  6. Day 06
    Story workshop, target-job build, rehearsal

    A storytelling workshop covers the demo-day pitch and interview framing, alongside the target-job build and a full rehearsal. Visit / fireside slot

  7. Day 07
    Demo day, with judges and awards

    The live pitch, in front of real founders and operators, with real awards.

The week

Seven days, four movements. Pre-work starts before day one.

Every framework is taught the hour before you use it: interviewing before real user interviews, pricing the day you go on sale, pitch anatomy the day before the stage.

Days 1 to 2. Research: a real problem, chosen well.

Theme selection or your own proposal, structured user research, real interviews, competitive teardown, a mini-PRD with prioritized features. You arrive with pre-work done and ideas forming.

User research · competitive analysis · PRD and prioritization

Days 3 to 5. Build and launch: product, brand, price.

AI coding agents build your product while you learn to direct them. Design system, landing page, advertisement, pricing. The build freezes at a fixed hour: what is live is what demos.

Coding agents · design and brand · launch and iterate on real feedback

Throughout. Career transfer: from product to offer letter.

Two company visits with debriefs, guest speakers and fireside panels from top companies, interview scenarios, and the target-job build. Every session answers the same question: how does this land me the role I want?

Company visits · guest firesides · interview scenarios · portfolio and GitHub

Days 6 to 7. The story and the stage: demo day.

Structured storytelling workshop, double-purposed: how you pitch on demo day and how you frame this work in every interview after. Then the live pitch to real founders and operators, with a real prize.

Story workshop · rehearsal · demo day with real judges
