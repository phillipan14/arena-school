Framework

One framework, every program.

The learner sits at the center. Four strands of work circle them. Four teaching principles hold it in place.

Four strands
  1. Inquiry and planningTurn an interest into a focused question, and plan the work.
  2. Research and evaluationCheck sources, including AI output, and choose on evidence.
  3. Creating and communicatingBuild and test it with AI, and explain it clearly.
  4. Collaboration and reflectionUse critique from teachers and peers to revise the work.
Four teaching principles
  1. In person, real-time feedback
  2. AI use disclosed and cited
  3. Learner voice and choice
  4. An authentic audience

What learners will be able to do

Outcomes you can assess on any project.

The same outcomes hold for every learner. The depth grows, from middle school to adults.

Inquiry and planning

  • Turn a broad interest into a focused, researchable question.
  • Plan a project with milestones, and adjust the plan based on progress.

Research and evaluation

  • Find, evaluate, and cite sources, including checking the accuracy of AI output.
  • Compare options against criteria, and justify a choice.
  • Explain how AI tools work, where they fail, and the bias and privacy issues they raise.

Creating and communicating

  • Build and test a product, prototype, or solution, deciding which steps to hand to AI and disclosing how they used it.
  • Structure an argument and write it clearly, in their own words.
  • Present to an authentic audience, and defend their reasoning under questioning.

Collaboration and reflection

  • Work in a team, share roles, and contribute to a shared product.
  • Give and use critique from teachers and peers to revise their work.
  • Reflect on their process and on what they would do differently.
Assessment

We assess the thinking behind the work.

The process, not only the product: how a learner chose, checked, and finished.

Sample criterion · Research and evaluation

Evaluates sources, including AI output

  1. BeginningAccepts sources and AI output as given; cites inconsistently.
  2. DevelopingChecks some claims against a second source; cites most sources.
  3. ProficientVerifies key claims, including AI output, against reliable sources; discloses AI use.
  4. ExtendingExplains why sources disagree, corrects AI errors, and judges which evidence to trust.

Used with students in the ISB AI Founders Program, summer 2026. With a school, the rubrics stay with its team.
