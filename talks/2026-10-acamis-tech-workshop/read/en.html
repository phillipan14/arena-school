Teaching philosophy

Staying competitive with AI takes three human skills.
Without them, AI does the thinking.

01Agency

Choosing what is worth doing, before anyone assigns it.

Without itAI picks the problem, and learners follow.

02Taste

Judging whether the output is actually good.

Without itLearners accept the first answer AI gives.

03Craft

Finishing the work so people can trust it.

Without itThe work stays an unchecked first draft.

This is how we teach every learner, from students to teachers and parents, and the lens for today.

Learning outcomes

Across every program, learners will be able to…

Learners are whoever a program is for: students, teachers, school leaders, or parents. The outcomes stay the same; the depth grows.

Inquiry and planning
  • Turn a broad interest into a focused, researchable question.
  • Plan a project with milestones, and adjust the plan based on progress.
Research and evaluation
  • Find, evaluate, and cite sources, including checking the accuracy of AI output.
  • Compare options against criteria, and justify a choice.
  • Explain how AI tools work, where they fail, and the bias and privacy issues they raise.
Creating and communicating
  • Build and test a product, prototype, or solution, deciding which steps to hand to AI and disclosing how they used it.
  • Structure an argument and write it clearly, in their own words.
  • Present to an authentic audience, and defend their reasoning under questioning.
Collaboration and reflection
  • Work in a team, share roles, and contribute to a shared product.
  • Give and use critique from teachers and peers to revise their work.
  • Reflect on their process and on what they would do differently.

How we assess

We assess the process, not only the product.

Sample criterion · research and evaluationEvaluates sources, including AI output
Beginning

Accepts sources and AI output as given; cites inconsistently.

Developing

Checks some claims against a second source; cites most sources.

Proficient

Verifies key claims, including AI output, against reliable sources; discloses AI use.

Extending

Explains why sources disagree, corrects AI errors, and judges which evidence to trust.

Evidence we collect
  • Process journal
  • Drafts
  • Recorded defense

What teachers ask us before a session

Teachers don’t lack ideas. They lack time, approved tools, and a place to start.

“We have no time for more PD.”

Start from one task you already do, and make the tool in the session.

“I’m not technical. Will I keep up?”

No coding: you describe the tool in plain English.

“What about students using AI to cheat?”

Assess the process: drafts, reflections, and the reasons behind each change.

“Our school restricts tools and student data.”

Use only the tools your school approves, with no student data in them.
