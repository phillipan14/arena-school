03. Your project can begin with your life, your community, or a wider issue.

Read slide text

People around you

Could you help a family member, make a service easier to use, or teach something people find confusing?

Your community

Could you support a charity, help neighbours share resources, or make information more accessible?

The wider world

Could you reduce waste, explain an environmental issue, or explore a better way to use energy?

Think of something you already care about. Who experiences the problem, and what would you like to change?

04. You can develop a first version while you are still exploring the idea.

Read slide text
Describe a meaningful idea

An oral-history archive, an arts exhibition, or a resource for your community.

→
Make an early version with AI

Explain what visitors should see and do. Ask for a draft you can try.

→
Use your subject knowledge to improve it

Check the interpretation, the experience, and whether it serves your audience.

Humanities and arts projects can become interactive experiences without starting from a coding course.

05. Imagine having help with several different parts of your project.

Read slide text
You direct the workPurpose, context, and decisions stay with your team.
Ask for research support

Find sources and prepare questions.

→
Use the evidence in a design brief

Explain what people should be able to do.

→
Review the draft and give feedback

Test the result; ask for specific revisions.

An agent can use tools to carry out several steps. A chatbot response alone may only be a draft or a piece of code.

06. The quality of your instructions changes the help you receive.

Read slide text
Too little context

“Make something about food waste.”

The tool has to guess the audience, the purpose, and the format.

A project brief the tool can use

“We want to help families understand what food they throw away. Help us design a simple one-week diary. It should take two minutes a day. Ask us what you need to know first.”

08. Choose the product that matches the work you want it to do.

Read slide text
ProductUseful starting taskImportant distinction
DeepSeek chatExplain a concept, discuss an approach, or draft code.Code in a reply is not the same as a tested, published app.
DeepSeek HarnessWork on files and run commands or tests in a project.A separate agent product in preview; requires setup.
Kimi Build / 3D MakeDevelop a website or explore a 3D model.Choose the dedicated mode; inspect the actual output.

A tool’s ability to accept images is different from its ability to write and execute code.

09. Canva can turn an idea into an interactive experience.

Read slide text
Describe the experience

A history quiz, an exhibition guide, or a food-waste diary.

→
Create and revise in Canva Code

Ask for the interaction, try it, and adjust the design.

→
Publish and gather responses

Use a website link; use Forms or supported Sheets connections for records.

UWC uses Canva. Check that Canva Code is enabled for student accounts; it is not supported on canva.cn.

11. A food-waste project could involve research, a diary, and a conversation.

12. AI can help you explain a device before you build the physical version.

Read slide text
Develop the concept

Ask AI to help compare sensors and sketch an enclosure.

→
Inspect the digital model

Check dimensions, fit, and exported files with a mentor.

→
Build and test the physical version

Connect the hardware and compare its readings with reality.

Example: a garden-watering reminder. A simulated reading or a 3D preview is not a working sensor.

13. A polished result is not evidence that the project works.

Read slide text
Try the result with someone

Can they understand the message or complete the task?

→
Compare it with evidence

Check sources, saved records, measurements, or observations.

→
Revise what did not work

Explain the failure and what a better version should do.

Keep a record of what you learned. Generated interviews and imagined test results cannot replace real evidence.

14. Explain what is wrong and ask for a specific revision.

Read slide text
First draft

A food diary asks families to enter the weight, price, and category of every item they throw away.

It might produce detailed data, but it asks too much of the people using it.

Your feedback

“This takes too long. Make a version with three simple entries per day. Explain what information we would lose, so we can decide whether that tradeoff is acceptable.”

15. Keep a record of the decisions behind the final result.

Read slide text
