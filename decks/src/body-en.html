Anthropic Economic Index, Jan 15, 2026: share of occupations using AI for at least 25% of their tasks (36% in the January 2025 report). The 2026 figure pools data across reports and reflects how people use Claude, not a census of every worker.

Doctors · a randomized trial in China

With AI, eye doctors got the diagnosis right 92% of the time. Without it, 75%.

The EyeFM trial · Nature Medicine

Patients given the correct diagnosis

Eye doctors without AI75.4%
Eye doctors with AI92.2%

Shanghai Jiao Tong University, Tsinghua University, and others, Nature Medicine, Aug 28, 2025: 668 patients and 16 eye doctors at one center, retinal screening; correct referrals also rose, from 80.5% to 92.2%

This is not about saving time. In a controlled trial, the doctors who worked with AI were simply right more often, and patients will choose those doctors. In a separate test in June 2025, Microsoft reported that its AI system solved 85% of very hard published cases, against 20% for experienced doctors working without help; that study is not yet peer-reviewed.

Accenture's workforce shrank by more than 11,000 in one quarter while it nearly doubled its AI and data staff to 77,000.

The how is no longer scarce: most new Google code is an AI draft that an engineer approves, and at Baidu, AI now generates 52% of new code, up from 20% in 2023 and 27% in 2024, according to Robin Li, who wants it at 80% to 90%. Writing and analysis follow the same path. The next slide is what becomes scarce instead.

Employers already treat judgment, creativity, resilience, and leadership as essential, and they expect those to keep rising. Microsoft’s users say the same in their own words: the job moves from generating answers to judging them.

Name the scarce skills

Agency chooses, taste judges, and craft finishes.

The three skills
  1. 01AgencyDecide what is worth building before anyone assigns it.
  2. 02TasteJudge whether the output is actually good.
  3. 03CraftFinish it so people can trust it.
Three names for what is now scarce. Agency is deciding what is worth building before anyone assigns it. Taste is judging whether the output is actually good, including the creative call. Craft is finishing it so people can trust it. Employers describe the same shift in their own words: the WEF lists creative thinking and leadership among the skills rising through 2030.

In a high school student’s week

What do agency, taste, and craft look like in practice?

Before

“Just give me the answer.”

The homework gets done. The thinking gets skipped.

Agency

Sees a younger brother stuck on fractions and builds him a practice game with AI, without being asked.

Taste

Builds a deck for a class talk and keeps reworking it, not just the design but the clearest way to say it.

Craft

Tests the club website on Grandma’s phone, fixes the tiny text and the broken sign-up form, then shares it.

Here is what the three skills look like in an ordinary week. Before: AI hands over the answer, the homework gets done, and the thinking gets skipped. Agency is seeing a problem nobody assigned, like a younger brother stuck on fractions, and making something for it. Taste is knowing when something is not good enough yet, like reworking a class presentation until the idea comes across clearly, not just until the slides look nice. Craft is testing the work on a real person, like a grandparent’s phone, and fixing what breaks before sharing it. None of this needs a special class. It needs a real reason to make something.

What children need

Skills grow from practice with structure.

  1. {{ic:folder-kanban}}A real projectwith something at stake
  2. {{ic:search-check}}A coachwho asks how you checked it
  3. {{ic:messages-square}}Honest feedbackfrom real people
That is how everyday AI use turns into agency, taste, and craft. The tools are everywhere. What is scarce is the structure around them: a real problem to solve, someone who asks how you checked it, and a reason to finish. That kind of practice is hard for any school to give every child yet, which is where families come in.

CNAES survey, 3,653 responses across eastern, central, and western China, May–June 2026, published Aug 19, 2026. In the US, 68% of teachers had no school or district training on AI (Gallup and Walton Family Foundation, 2025).

Beijing now requires at least 8 class hours of AI a year, a real start. Teachers everywhere are still learning these tools themselves, and a few hours a year can’t give every child all the practice these skills need. That is where families can help, which is the next part.

This is what the three skills look like with AI tools in a child’s hands. An 11-year-old in Hong Kong, summer 2025, had five days. He chose his own problem, sign language to speech, because his cousin is deaf. Nobody assigned it. That is agency.
