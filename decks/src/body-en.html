A question to start

Think back to your first job. What did you learn that first year?

For most people, the first job is where judgment is learned: small, routine tasks, done again and again, with someone checking the work.

More and more of that routine work is now done with AI’s help. So where will young people learn judgment?

01 · AI-native work

AI is changing jobs more than removing them.

1 in 4

workers worldwide are in jobs exposed to generative AI. The most likely effect is that the job changes.

ILO and NASK, May 2025
39%

of workers’ core skills are expected to change by 2030, down from 44% two years earlier.

World Economic Forum, Future of Jobs 2025
170M vs 92M

jobs expected to be created vs displaced by 2030, a net gain of 78 million.

World Economic Forum, Future of Jobs 2025

The question for children today is less “will there be work?” and more “what will the work ask of them?”

Finding one exam date across dozens of folders.

A student’s course files are scattered across dozens of Blackboard folders. Finding one late policy or exam date means clicking through every course, and the answer is usually buried deep in a PDF.

By hand

Click through every course and week folder, open PDFs one by one, and skim for the line.

AI-native work · Example 2 · for a job search
See it live→arenaschool.org/case-study-examples

Walking into an interview prepared.

A candidate has a few days before an interview. Turning one résumé and one job posting into likely questions, rehearsed answers, and a study plan takes hours, and twice as long if they practice in two languages.

By hand

Hours of rereading, guessing questions, and writing answers, then doing it again in a second language.

Just a chatbot

Generic answers, one section at a time, and nothing to practice with or track.

With this tool

One upload gives a full bilingual kit tied to your résumé, editable by asking, on your own key.

What it actually took

The skill is not coding. It is knowing what you want.

1Know, generally, what outcome you want
2If needed, brainstorm with your coding agent first
3Ask it directly what it needs from you
4Once there’s a first output, feed it feedback like crazy

You don’t need to know how it’s built. You need to know what it is when it’s done.

Talk through the best way to implement it and the caveats.

“What information do you need from me to begin the build?”

Treat yourself as a product manager giving feedback to a team member who takes instructions very literally.

These are habits anyone can practice, at any age.

02 · What employers look for

The first job now asks for judgment earlier.

7×

Entry-level roles that use AI are seven times more likely to ask for skills once expected of senior people, like judgment and leadership.

PwC Global AI Jobs Barometer, June 2026
+35%

growth in those AI-exposed entry-level roles since 2019, while other entry-level roles fell 10%.

PwC Global AI Jobs Barometer, June 2026

Young people who use AI well, and can judge what it gives them, are trusted with bigger decisions sooner.

At work today

Candidates now show what they can do.

What we’re seeing in tech companies
  • Everyone prototypes, not only engineers. A marketer mocks up a tool before asking for one.
  • The first draft is cheap. The discussion is about which version is right.
  • People are judged on what they decided, and why.
How Jing Jing evaluated non-technical candidates at Kimi

When Jing Jing was hiring for Kimi’s growth team, one candidate built a small tool for the team’s social media work before the interview, showed it live, and got the offer. Nobody asked for it. That was the point.

04 · Skills that compound

Agency, taste, and craft.

When AI can make almost anything, these are the skills that compound: knowing what’s worth making, whether it’s good, and how to finish it.

Agency

Choosing what’s worth solving.

Every useful tool starts with deciding a task is worth fixing.

At workTasks that only follow instructions are the easiest to hand to AI; people who define the problem direct the work.

Taste

Telling which version actually works.

AI offers many versions; the work is asking which one people will actually use.

At workTeams can generate ten versions in an hour; the person who picks the right one, and can say why, is the one they rely on.

Craft

Finishing it well.

The first draft comes fast. Making something people can rely on takes care.

At workWorking products earn trust in a way prototypes and slide decks do not.

“People must be good at deciding what to do and why.”

Small things families can do, without adding another class

Make one small thing together.

A family chore list, a trip plan, or a quiz for a younger sibling.

Ask, “How did you check it?”

Treat AI’s answer as a first draft, and talk about what was wrong with it.

Show it to a real person.

A grandparent, a teacher, or a friend. Real feedback is where taste comes from.
