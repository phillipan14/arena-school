The question behind today’s talk

When AI can draft an essay in seconds, what must our children still learn to do?

Nobody can predict exact jobs in 2040. But we can read what is already happening, and the direction is clear.

One number for scale, then three careers parents trust: doctors, lawyers, and bankers. As Mu-ming Poo of the Chinese Academy of Sciences put it in May 2025, the coming decades are not about AI replacing people, but about people who use AI replacing people who don't.

Anthropic Economic Index · based on real use of Claude

In about half of all jobs, AI is already used for at least a quarter of the tasks.

Anthropic Economic Index, Jan 15, 2026: share of occupations using AI for at least 25% of their tasks (36% in the January 2025 report). The 2026 figure pools data across reports and reflects how people use Claude, not a census of every worker.

This is measured from millions of real conversations with Claude. In a single year the share rose from about a third of jobs to about half.

Doctors · a randomized trial in China

With AI, eye doctors got the diagnosis right 92% of the time. Without it, 75%.

The EyeFM trial · Nature Medicine

Patients given the correct diagnosis

Eye doctors without AI75.4%
Eye doctors with AI92.2%

Shanghai Jiao Tong University, Tsinghua University, and others, Nature Medicine, Aug 28, 2025: 668 patients and 16 eye doctors at one centre, retinal screening; correct referrals also rose, from 80.5% to 92.2%

This is not about saving time. In a controlled trial, the doctors who worked with AI were simply right more often, and patients will choose those doctors. In a separate test in June 2025, Microsoft reported that its AI system solved 85% of very hard published cases, against 20% for experienced doctors working without help; that study is not yet peer-reviewed.

Who stays · from the same memo, June 2025

“Those who embrace this change, become conversant in AI … will be well-positioned to have high impact.”

Andy Jassy, CEO of AmazonMemo to employees, June 17, 2025
His advice to his own staff: be curious about AI, educate yourself, take the trainings, and use AI whenever you can. That is advice for our children too. Other employers say the same thing, so they stay in these notes: Accenture is exiting staff for whom reskilling is not a viable path (Julie Sweet, Sept 25, 2025); Microsoft told every role that using AI is no longer optional (Julia Liuson, June 2025); Duolingo made AI use part of performance reviews (Luis von Ahn, April 28, 2025). Accenture's workforce shrank by more than 11,000 in one quarter while it nearly doubled its AI and data staff to 77,000.

Employers already treat judgment, creativity, resilience, and leadership as essential, and they expect those to keep rising. Microsoft’s users say the same in their own words: the job moves from generating answers to judging them.

Name the scarce skills

Agency, taste, and craft, plus creativity and leadership, each have a job.

  1. 01AgencyDecide what is worth building before anyone assigns it
  2. 02TasteJudge whether the output is actually good
  3. 03CraftFinish it so people can trust it
  4. 04CreativityFind a new answer when the obvious one is cheap
  5. 05LeadershipDirect people and AI toward the same goal
Three names we use, plus the two employers already pay for. Agency is deciding and leading. Taste is judging, including the creative call. Craft is finishing. Creativity and leadership are how the WEF writes the same shift.

How children use it · OECD, PISA 2025

The same AI can hand a child the answer or help them understand it. Habits decide which.

Shortcut

“Just give me the answer.”

The homework gets done. The thinking gets skipped.

Tutor

“Explain it until I get it.”

The child does the thinking. AI explains and checks.

OECD, PISA 2025 results, Sept 8, 2026: students who used AI chatbots for schoolwork scored lower on average, but frequent users who had been taught to judge AI output did better. A correlation, not proof of cause

Same tool, opposite result. The difference is the habit, and habits are built at home.

What children need

Skills grow from practice with structure.

  1. {{ic:folder-kanban}}A real projectwith something at stake
  2. {{ic:search-check}}A coachwho asks how you checked it
  3. {{ic:messages-square}}Honest feedbackfrom real people
That is how everyday AI use turns into agency, taste, and craft, and it is how our programs are built. The tools are everywhere. What is scarce is the structure around them: a real problem to solve, someone who asks how you checked it, and a reason to finish. Schools cannot yet give every child that practice, which is why the next part is what families can do now.

CNAES survey, 3,653 responses across eastern, central, and western China, May–June 2026, published Aug 19, 2026. In the US, 68% of teachers had no school or district training on AI (Gallup and Walton Family Foundation, 2025).

This is not a criticism of teachers. 44% of schools say they have severely too few. Beijing now requires at least 8 class hours of AI a year, a real start, and 81% of teachers in a national survey have tried AI themselves. A few hours a year, and teachers still learning the tools, cannot give every child the practice these skills need. That is the handoff: what families can do now.
