A question to start

Think back to your first job. What did you learn that first year?

For most people, the first job is where judgment is learned: small, routine tasks, done again and again, with someone checking the work.

More and more of that routine work is now done with AI’s help. So where will young people learn judgment?

01 · The jobs

People in these jobs already work with a tool.

Law

Loan reviews at A&O Shearman

With Harvey, the firm’s lawyers built tools for antitrust filings, cybersecurity, fund formation, and loan review. Across a portfolio, the loan tool compares agreements, and a lawyer oversees it.

A&O Shearman, April 6, 2025
Medicine

Doctors at a Hefei hospital

In a 2025 pilot with Xunfei Healthcare (讯飞医疗), an outpatient visit becomes a draft note the doctor can cite. The suggested diagnosis shows its reasoning, so the doctor can check it.

Anhui Medical University First Affiliated Hospital, June 4, 2025
Finance

Commercial & Investment Bank

More than 65,000 people in that part of the bank actively use LLM Suite, JPMorgan’s own AI platform. It is there for drafting and for coming up with ideas.

JPMorganChase, June 3, 2025 and April 6, 2026
Consulting

Consultants at McKinsey

Two-thirds of employees use Lilli, the firm’s own AI platform, at least monthly, and more than 40% use it weekly. It can draft slides and rewrite text in the firm’s style. Consultants check the result.

The Boston Globe, July 31, 2025
Software

Engineers at Google, and Copilot

Well over 30% of the code checked in at Google includes an AI suggestion that an engineer chose to accept. GitHub Copilot, a tool engineers use at many companies, now has 50 million users.

Alphabet, April 24, 2025 · Microsoft, July 29, 2026
02 · Hiring

Some teams are smaller. The wider change is in the work.

Salesforce

Support, from 9,000 to about 5,000

Talking about the company’s AI support agents, Marc Benioff said the support team went from 9,000 people to about 5,000, and that he was shifting roles toward sales. Salesforce said hundreds of employees moved into other jobs, and that it is no longer filling every open support role.

The Logan Bartlett Show, Aug 29, 2025 · The Register, Sept 2, 2025
Accenture

Training first. Some exits where it will not reach.

Accenture said training people is still its main response. Where reskilling is not a viable path, it is letting people go on a faster timeline. In that quarter, $344 million of the charge was severance. It also expects the overall number of employees to rise in fiscal 2026.

Accenture earnings release, Sept 25, 2025
Early-career hiring

What a wider survey found

11% of employers were discussing whether some positions might go. More than two-thirds were discussing how AI fits tasks inside a job. More than half said AI has not reduced the tasks entry-level workers do. Just over a quarter said those tasks have shrunk.

NACE, April 20, 2026

People are being affected, which is why the tools are worth knowing. Most of the change is in the work itself.

02 · New graduates

What employers ask of interns and new graduates.

United States

AI skills, in the posting and the internship

More than one-third of entry-level jobs now require AI skills, nearly triple the share in fall 2025. Nearly 60% of employers assign interns projects that use AI tools. 28% say they are looking for early-career people who can use AI in the work.

NACE Job Outlook, April 20, 2026
Ten countries

Check the output. Think it through.

Among 20,000 knowledge workers who already use AI, the human skills they ranked higher were quality control of AI output (50%) and critical thinking (46%). 86% said they treat the output as a starting point and stay responsible for the thinking.

Microsoft Work Trend Index, 2026
China

AI tools, and the thinking around them

Besides professional skills, 35.3% of employers value being able to use AI tools. 76.5% value data analysis and visualization, and 29.4% value critical thinking. Employers could name more than one.

解放日报, via 上观新闻, March 27, 2026

What they are asking for shows up next as agency, taste, and craft: choosing the work, telling whether the result is good, and finishing it.

A hiring story

Candidates now show what they can do.

What we’re seeing in tech companies
  • Everyone prototypes, not only engineers. A marketer mocks up a tool before asking for one.
  • The first draft is cheap. The discussion is about which version is right.
  • People are judged on what they decided, and why.
How Jing Jing evaluated non-technical candidates at Kimi

When Jing Jing was hiring for Kimi’s growth team, one candidate built a small tool for the team’s social media work before the interview, showed it live, and got the offer. Nobody asked for it. That was the point.

From teaching

Three lessons from the classroom.

01
Agency

Choosing is the hard part.

Students pick up the tools in an afternoon. Choosing a problem worth solving takes longer, and matters more.

02
Taste

Real feedback makes it better.

Live feedback from real users (a classmate, a colleague, or an instructor) is how students improve faster, and how a working product becomes a better one.

03
Craft

Finishing is where they grow.

The first version comes fast. Finishing it so others can rely on it is where students grow the most.

04 · Skills that compound

Agency, taste, and craft.

Employers are already asking for this. When AI can make almost anything, these are the skills that compound: knowing what’s worth making, whether it’s good, and how to finish it.

Agency

Choosing what’s worth solving.

Every useful tool starts with deciding a task is worth fixing.

At workTasks that only follow instructions are the easiest to hand to AI; people who define the problem direct the work.

Taste

Telling which version actually works.

AI offers many versions; the work is asking which one people will actually use.

At workTeams can generate ten versions in an hour; the person who picks the right one, and can say why, is the one they rely on.

Craft

Finishing it well.

The first draft comes fast. Making something people can rely on takes care.

At workWorking products earn trust in a way prototypes and slide decks do not.

“People must be good at deciding what to do and why.”
Cornell University, Report of the Committee on the Future of the American University, September 2026

Small things families can do, without adding another class

Make one small thing together.

A family chore list, a trip plan, or a quiz for a younger sibling.

Ask, “How did you check it?”

Treat AI’s answer as a first draft, and talk about what was wrong with it.

Show it to a real person.

A grandparent, a teacher, or a friend. Real feedback is where taste comes from.
