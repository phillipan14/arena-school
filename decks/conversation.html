The question behind today’s talk

When AI can draft an essay in seconds, what must our children still learn to do?

The answer starts with what is already happening at work.

Nobody can predict exact jobs in 2040. But we can read what is already happening, and the direction is clear.

The careers parents hope for

The safest careers are changing too.

In medicine, law, banking, and engineering, the people who work well with AI are already pulling ahead.

  1. 1DoctorsA randomized trial in China
  2. 2LawyersA US trial and China’s law firms
  3. 3BankersChina’s biggest bank

For decades these were the safe answers. The next four slides show what is at stake in each: who does better work, how many people are needed, and who gets hired. As the neuroscientist Mu-ming Poo of the Chinese Academy of Sciences put it in May 2025, the coming decades are not about AI replacing people, but about people who use AI replacing people who don't.

1 of 4 · Doctors · a randomized trial in China

In a randomized trial, eye doctors working with AI got the diagnosis right 92% of the time. Without AI, 75%.

The EyeFM trial · Nature Medicine

Patients given the correct diagnosis

Eye doctors without AI75.4%
Eye doctors with AI92.2%

Shanghai Jiao Tong University, Tsinghua University, and others, Nature Medicine, Aug 28, 2025: 668 patients and 16 eye doctors at one centre, retinal screening; correct referrals also rose, from 80.5% to 92.2%

This is not about saving time. In a controlled trial, the doctors who worked with AI were simply right more often, and patients will choose those doctors. In a separate test in June 2025, Microsoft reported that its AI system solved 85% of very hard published cases, against 20% for experienced doctors working without help; that study is not yet peer-reviewed.

In a randomized trial, law students using AI got 50% to 130% more done on most legal tasks.

Law students are the right comparison for our children: the ones who worked with AI simply got more done, and mostly did better work. The trial also caught AI making mistakes, which is why judging AI's work matters. In China, nearly every lawyer already uses AI, so not using it is the risk. And at the largest US firms, fewer new graduates were hired in 2025, for the first time in over a decade.

Engineers are not disappearing, but each one now does more, so companies need fewer of them for the same work. In the US, SignalFire found new-graduate hiring at the biggest tech firms down by more than half since 2019, though not only because of AI. In China, demand has moved to people who build with AI, while hiring for basic Java and iOS jobs has dropped.

Putting it together

Everything so far points to three skills: agency, taste, and craft.

  1. 01Agency

    Choosing what’s worth solving

    Deciding what to make before anyone asks

  2. 02Taste

    Telling which version actually works

    Checking AI’s work before anyone relies on it

  3. 03Craft

    Finishing it well

    Turning a first draft into something people trust

Agency is what the employers who reward initiative are paying for. Taste is the 50% who put checking AI's output first. Craft is finishing: the part AI's first draft never does.

CNAES survey, 3,653 responses across eastern, central, and western China, May–June 2026, published Aug 19, 2026. In the US, 68% of teachers had no school or district training on AI (Gallup and Walton Family Foundation, 2025).

This is not a criticism of teachers. Beijing now requires at least 8 class hours of AI a year, a real start, and 81% of teachers in a national survey have tried AI themselves. But a few hours a year and teachers still learning the tools cannot give every child the practice these skills need.

How children use it · OECD, PISA 2025

The same AI can hand a child the answer or help them understand it. Habits decide which.

Shortcut

“Just give me the answer.”

The homework gets done. The thinking gets skipped.

Tutor

“Explain it until I get it.”

The child does the thinking. AI explains and checks.

OECD, PISA 2025 results, Sept 8, 2026: students who used AI chatbots for schoolwork scored lower on average, but frequent users who had been taught to judge AI output did better. A correlation, not proof of cause

Same tool, opposite result. The difference is the habit, and habits are built at home.

What children need

Skills grow from practice with structure: a real project, a coach who asks how you checked it, and honest feedback.

That is how everyday AI use turns into agency, taste, and craft, and it is how our programs are built.

The tools are everywhere. What is scarce is the structure around them: a real problem to solve, someone who asks how you checked it, and a reason to finish.

Takeaway 2 · at home

Start three habits at home this week.

  1. 1Agency

    Make one real thing together

    A chore chart, a trip plan, or a quiz for a younger sibling

  2. 2Taste

    Ask, “How did you check it?”

    Treat every AI answer as a first draft

  3. 3Craft

    Show it to a real person

    A grandparent, a teacher, or a friend

Each habit trains one of the three skills. None needs special software, and each takes minutes.

Takeaway 3 · then and now

When we were young, most ideas stayed in a notebook. Today a child can use AI to turn one into something real.

When we were young
  • Almost everything was done by hand
  • Building software took a team and years of training
  • Few people outside the family ever saw what you made
Today
  • With AI as a helper, a first working version comes together quickly
  • Real people can try it the same week
  • A child’s idea can reach real users before they leave school
We wrote essays by hand and waited years to build anything real. Our children can build, test, and improve in a week. The ones who start now compound that advantage.

Takeaway 3 · why start now

Any AI tool takes an afternoon to learn. The three skills take years, so start now.

Tools change every few months. Choosing a problem, judging the result, and finishing the work carry into any job, and a few years’ head start adds up.

A child who starts at twelve has years of practice by the first job. That head start is the comparative advantage.

If you remember one thing

When AI can make almost anything, students need to know what’s worth making, whether it’s good, and how to finish it.

The children who start building now will pull ahead. There has never been a better time to begin.
