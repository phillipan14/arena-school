Our approach

Agency. Taste. Craft.

When AI can make almost anything, students need to know what’s worth making, whether it’s good, and how to finish it.

  • 01 · AgencyChoosing what’s worth solving
  • 02 · TasteTelling which version works
  • 03 · CraftFinishing it well
Three pillars

How each pillar is practiced.

Students practice all three on projects they choose, with mentors in the room.

Agency

Choosing what’s worth solving.

AI can help make almost anything, so the real work is deciding what’s worth making. Students choose their own project on day one.

  • Pitch their own idea in three minutes, no slides.
  • Ask an expert for fifteen minutes of advice.
  • Set a personal goal to keep for ninety days.
Principles I · V · VI
  1. IAgency from day one. Mentors support; students choose.
  2. VReflection, started early. Thirty screen-free minutes a day on one question: which of my goals are my own?
  3. VIRisk early, while it’s cheap. Small, repeated risks make larger ones easier.

Taste

Telling which version actually works.

AI helps students try many versions. They learn which one works by showing their work to users and practitioners every day.

  • Put a prototype online at a public link by midway.
  • Work through the product with an experienced mentor.
  • Test it with five questions, then defend each answer.
Principles II · III · VIII
  1. IIMake things in front of people. Every day ends with a demo.
  2. IIIExperienced mentors in the room. Mentors are founders, engineers, and designers who have made real products.
  3. VIIIFirst-principles thinking. Students are assessed on asking how, why, what principle, when it fails, and how to adapt.

Craft

Finishing it well.

AI helps students reach a first draft sooner; finishing a reliable product is their own work. Every build ends in a live demo.

  • Build with AI coding tools, making every call.
  • Stop at a fixed freeze and demo what works.
  • Write a one-page personal routine to take home.
Principles IV · VII
  1. IVAI as a partner students direct. Students write the prompts, question the output, and check it for mistakes.
  2. VIIDaily habits, personal systems. A three-line update every day: finished, learned, next.
A typical day

One day in a cohort, start to finish.

Lessons arrive right before students need them, and every day ends with a standup.

  1. StartLesson

    Taught right before students need it.

  2. Most of the dayBuild

    On a problem the team chose.

  3. During the buildMentor 1:1s

    Mentors sit with teams when they are stuck.

  4. Fixed hourThe freeze

    Building stops; teams demo what works.

  5. DailyReflection

    Thirty minutes with a notebook, no screens.

  6. End of dayStandup

    Finished, learned, and next.

  7. Last dayDemo day

    A live pitch to practitioners and parents.
