Our approach

Agency, taste, and craft.

When AI can make almost anything, learners need to know what is worth making, whether it is good, and how to finish it.

Find the right program →Read the learning approach
Three skills

What AI cannot do for them.

Each skill gets a moment in every program that learners cannot skip.

Agency

Choose what is worth doing, before anyone assigns it.

How learners practice it
Each learner chooses the problem and pitches it in three minutes, with no slides.
Without it
AI picks the problem, and the learner follows.
What it changes
  1. In schoolWhen AI can finish the assignment, the harder skill is setting the question.
  2. At workWork that only follows instructions is the easiest to hand to AI. People who define the problem direct the work.
  3. Over a careerA project a learner chose and saw through shows initiative a transcript cannot.

Taste

Judge whether the work is actually good.

How learners practice it
A working version goes live at a public link, and real users and mentors react every day.
Without it
The first answer AI gives becomes the final one.
What it changes
  1. In schoolWhen every essay can look polished, telling strong work from weak is what teachers need to see.
  2. At workA team can generate ten versions in an hour. The person who picks the right one, and says why, is the one they rely on.
  3. Over a careerJudgment compounds. It earns trust with larger decisions.

Craft

Finish the work so people can trust it.

How learners practice it
Building stops at a fixed hour, and on demo day learners explain every decision live.
Without it
The work stays an unchecked first draft.
What it changes
  1. In schoolA first draft is now instant. Revising it into something reliable is where the learning happens.
  2. At workA working product earns trust that a prototype or a slide deck does not.
  3. Over a careerA record of finished work, with a live link, speaks for itself.
Principles

How we teach.

Four principles in every room, a clear split of the work, and a day with the same shape every time.

  1. In person, real-time feedback

    Mentors sit with learners while they build, ask how they checked it, and respond on the spot.

  2. AI use disclosed and cited

    Learners decide which steps to hand to AI, and say how they used it.

  3. Learner voice and choice

    Learners choose the problem. Mentors support. They do not assign.

  4. An authentic audience

    Work goes in front of real users, judges, and families.

Who does what
AI tools and mentors handle
  • Writing the code
  • Putting it online
  • Fixing technical errors
Learners decide
  • Which problem, and for whom
  • What to cut after users try it
  • The name, the price, and the pitch
Eight principles, grouped by skill
Agency
  1. IAgency from day one.Mentors support; learners choose.
  2. VReflection, started early.Thirty screen-free minutes a day: which of my goals are my own?
  3. VIRisk early, while it’s cheap.Small, repeated risks make larger ones easier.
Taste
  1. IIMake things in front of people.Every day ends with a demo.
  2. IIIExperienced mentors in the room.Founders, engineers, and designers who have made real products.
  3. VIIIFirst-principles thinking.How, why, what principle, when it fails, and how to adapt.
Craft
  1. IVAI as a partner learners direct.Learners write the prompts, question the output, and check it.
  2. VIIDaily habits, personal systems.Three lines every day: finished, learned, next.
The shape of a day

The same rhythm in every format, from weekend sessions to five-day intensives.

  1. StartLessonTaught right before learners need it.
  2. Most of the dayBuildOn a problem the team chose.
  3. During the buildMentor 1:1sWhen a team is stuck.
  4. Fixed hourThe freezeBuilding stops. Teams demo what works.
  5. DailyReflectionThirty minutes, a notebook, no screens.
  6. End of dayStandupFinished, learned, next.
  7. Last dayDemo dayA live pitch to judges and families.
