ARENA SCHOOLCurriculum summary

Agency. Taste. Craft.

When AI can make almost anything, students need to know what's worth making, whether it's good, and how to finish it.

Students build their own AI products with practitioners in the room, and show the work to real people every day. Eight principles shape how each program runs; they group under three pillars.

The three pillars and eight principles

Agency

Choosing what's worth solving.
IAgency from day one

Students choose their own project on day one. Mentors support; students decide.

VReflection, started early

Thirty screen-free minutes a day on one question: which of my goals are my own?

VIRisk early, while it's cheap

Pitch without slides, cold-email an expert, show unfinished work. Small risks make larger ones easier.

Leave with: a signed 90-day promise to self.

Taste

Telling which version works.
IIMake things in front of people

Build, present, take feedback, revise. Every day ends with a demo; a public URL by midway.

IIIPractitioners in the room

Founders, engineers and designers who have shipped products review code and designs.

VIIIFirst-principles thinking

Five questions: how, why, what principle, when does it fail, how do I adapt. Assessed on asking them.

Leave with: professionals who know them by name.

Craft

Finishing it well.
IVAI is leverage, not a crutch

Prompting, AI coding agents, and critiquing output daily: catching errors and hallucinations.

VIIDaily habits, personal systems

A three-line standup every day (shipped, learned, next); every task has one owner.

•The freeze

At a fixed hour the build stops. Teams demo what works and learn to cut scope.

Leave with: a working product and a one-page personal routine.
