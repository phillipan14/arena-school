Fair questions

The doubts we hear, answered straight.

I’m not technical. Will I be left behind?+

The sessions assume nothing. If you can explain a task to a student teacher, you can do this. The most skeptical arrivals tend to leave with the most.

Won’t AI feedback feel generic to my students?+

Generic output comes from generic prompting, which is what we train out. Your rubric, your tone, every comment reviewed by you before it goes out.

My students use AI to cheat. Doesn’t this make it worse?+

Pretending the tools don’t exist is what makes it worse. We teach assessment design for a world where students have AI too, and teachers who understand the tools set better assignments than teachers who ban them.

Our school restricts which tools we can use.+

We adapt to whatever your school has approved. The skill is describing your task precisely; it isn’t tied to one product.

Will any of this survive next term?+

Tools built on your real workload get used next week, because the workload comes back. You keep every prompt and template, and school-wide trainings include follow-up.

The hours you get back go straight to the part of teaching that matters.
