# One-pager rethink (RETHINK-BRIEF, Oct 18, 2026)

A first-principles restart, not another tweak. Concepts only; nothing shipped.
Sources: sell-sheet and one-pager guidance (Gamma's one-pager guides), YC's inverted-pyramid advice for pitches (Garry
Tan, via a talk summary), capability-statement practice (Spark Interact, The Neuron), and the product-sheet style of
Stripe and Linear (one idea, one visual, restraint). Built on ONEPAGER-POSITIONING.md and Phillip's feedback rounds.

## Principles (what great one-pagers do)
1. **One big idea, stated literally.** The headline says what the thing is in plain words; a reader who stops there
   still knows it. ("Hands-on AI programs where students build and launch a real product.")
2. **Five-second scan path.** Headline, one hero visual, the outcome, the next step. Everything else is for the reader who
   chose to keep going. Design the path first, then fill it.
3. **Conclusion first, proof second** (YC's inverted pyramid). Outcomes before process; process before credentials.
4. **Proof over adjectives.** Named schools, named partners, a real artifact, a real photo. No "world-class", no
   "transformative". Logos and facts, not claims.
5. **One strong visual, not five weak ones.** A single real photo or a single diagram carries the page; other visuals
   are small and subordinate.
6. **A restrained type scale.** Three sizes do the work (display, body, label). Fewer greys, fewer weights. Contrast
   comes from size and space, not from more styles.
7. **Write for one audience at a time.** A parent page answers a parent's questions in a parent's words; it does not
   explain procurement or staff training.
8. **Structure the how as a picture.** A day-by-day strip or a ladder beats a paragraph about "our approach".
9. **Credibility sits close to the brand.** Memberships beside the wordmark; the team row as one calm, balanced block.
10. **One clear next step**, with a person and an address. One page, one action.

## Briefs per audience
### Parent
- **Motivations:** college applications; real AI skills; worry that school is not preparing their child for AI-era work;
  a path to internships and jobs.
- **The one thing to remember:** in five days my child builds and launches a real product, and keeps it.
- **Objections:** "Is this just screen time / prompting?" "Will it help with college?" "Who is teaching my child?"
  "Is it safe?"
- **Proof that answers them:** a working product at a public link, a documented record for applications (site wording);
  demo day in front of a panel (ISB, 2026); mentors from Harvard, Caltech, Tsinghua, McKinsey, Goldman Sachs, Y Combinator;
  Anthropic Startup Program and OpenAI Partner Network; the five-day structure; the no-promise admissions line.
- **CTA:** ask your child's school to bring Arena, or enroll in year-long mentorship.

### Teacher / school
- **Motivations:** not falling behind; whole-staff confidence, not a few early adopters; safe, compliant use with student
  data; time back; tools that work in class tomorrow.
- **The one thing to remember:** every teacher moves up one level a term, inside our school's rules.
- **Objections:** "My staff aren't technical." "Student data." "One more PD day that changes nothing."
- **Proof:** each teacher leaves a session with a working tool for their own class; five fluency levels with a baseline;
  staff training at Schwarzman College, Tsinghua; student data stays in school-approved accounts.
- **CTA:** book a planning call; run one session first.

### General
- **Motivations:** understand what Arena is in one glance and route themselves to the right program.
- **The one thing to remember:** hands-on AI programs where people build real things, for students, educators,
  families, and early career.
- **Objections:** "Is this another AI course?" "Who are you?"
- **Proof:** where we have run it (ISB, Bodley Academy, Schwarzman College), the team, the memberships.
- **CTA:** find your program; contact us.

## Concepts for the parent page (EN)

- **(a) Poster: "Demo day".** The page is the moment of payoff: the demo-day photo fills the top half, the literal
  headline sits on it, and one line names the hero project (a sign-language-to-speech app launched in five days at Bodley

- **(b) "A week at Arena".** The page is the five days themselves: a filmstrip of real photos, Day 1 to Day 5, each with

- **(c) Editorial: "What your child walks away with".** The page is a magazine spread about outcomes: a large serif
  headline, four numbered outcomes as the body copy, a narrow sidebar with the week and the one stat, one photo.

## Self-critique of the three concepts
- **(a) Poster.** Strongest first impression and the most emotional: a real student on stage carries the page, and the
  hero project makes "a real product" concrete. Weaknesses: the headline is a slogan rather than a literal description;
  the five-day structure is absent (Phillip asked for it); the one story is from Hong Kong under a Beijing photo, so the two
  must stay clearly labelled.
- **(b) A week at Arena.** The clearest "how": five real photos, one verb a day, ending on demo day, then the outcomes as
  the destination. Weaknesses: process leads and outcomes follow, against Phillip's "outcomes lead"; some day photos are
  illustrative (real Arena sessions, not that exact day), which needs honest captions before shipping.
- **(c) Editorial.** Outcomes lead with a literal, large headline; four numbered outcomes as the body; the one stat and
  the five days in a calm sidebar; one photo. It meets the most rules at once (outcomes first, literal, one stat, day-by-day,
  restraint) and reads as premium. Weaknesses: the least visual of the three; the photo is of mentors, not students.

## Recommendation
**Build the parent page on (c), the editorial, and borrow from (a) and (b):** keep its outcomes-first structure, large
serif headline, and sidebar, but swap its photo for the demo-day student photo from (a), and render the sidebar's five days
as a compact version of (b)'s filmstrip (five small real photos with one verb each). That gives a literal headline and
outcomes in the first five seconds, one strong human image, and the week as a picture. Then apply the same frame to the
teacher page (outcome: every teacher up one level a term; structure: the session flow) and the general page.
