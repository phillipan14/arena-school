Bodley Academy, Hong Kong.

Twenty students. Five days. One ten-year-old who built something nobody assigned him.

Bodley Academy · Rhodes House at Oxford · Hong Kong · Summer 2025 · Students track

The setup

Twenty students, five days, Bodley Academy at Rhodes House, Oxford.

Arena's first cohort ran in the summer of 2025, in Hong Kong, in partnership with Bodley Academy at Rhodes House at Oxford. Twenty students walked in for five days. The premise was simple: hand each of them a real problem and a mentor who knows their name, then get out of the way.

What students built

A ten-year-old walked in not knowing what an API was.

Five days later he demoed a working app that turned sign language into spoken words, something he built because his cousin is deaf. No one assigned it. The morning tutor had cleared enough of the floor that his afternoon was free to chase a real problem, and a mentor who knew his name stayed in the room until it worked.

He kept iterating after the demo was over, because the first version wasn't good enough for his grandmother to actually use it. The hardest part wasn't the model. It was getting the audio clean enough for an elderly woman to trust it.

The demo day

A pitch competition judged by real founders and operators, with a real prize.

The week ended on a stage: a pitch competition judged by real founders and operators, with a real prize on the line. One team walked away with the pitch winners' check, US$1,980 in real seed money.

That team came back from the demo evening having already spent the check on a real domain name, because they had every intention of keeping the company running.

What happened after

Two students walked out with letters.

One is now at Stanford. The other is at a top UK boarding school. Across the twenty students in the cohort, the pilot closed at a 9.5/10 Net Promoter Score.
